Wasp 1.0 is officially scoped. The feature set is decided, the work is broken down into issues on a [public roadmap](https://github.com/orgs/wasp-lang/projects/5), and we are targeting roughly six months. (Yes, that is a developer estimate. We padded it.)

More important than the date is what 1.0 actually means, so let's start there.

{/* truncate */}

## What 1.0 means for your app

1.0 is not a feature list. It is a promise about stability.

From 1.0 onward:

- **The Wasp API stops moving.** We follow semantic versioning. Your app keeps working across every minor and patch release.
- **Breaking changes require a major version**, and every one of them ships with a migration guide.
- **Core dependencies stay current.** TanStack Query, Prisma and the rest will never be more than six months behind upstream: up to date, without the bleeding-edge hiccups.

### Already building on Wasp? Don't wait for 1.0.

We are not holding these improvements back for one big release. We ship them as we merge them, which means you get them as they land and you will be hearing from us a lot over the next six months. When the last one is in, we call it 1.0.

So there is nothing to wait for. Start now, upgrade as you go, and every breaking change between here and 1.0 comes with a migration guide, same as always.

## Beta gave us the freedom to explore

Being in Beta let us explore unhindered by the weight and responsibility of maintaining a >=1.0 framework. We used all of it.

We never had an exact feature set that would make 1.0. What we had was a clear idea of what we wanted: the fullstackiest managed experience of building web apps there is. Writing only the code you care about, at the level of detail you want, with the rest taken care of for you. All in code, flexible, free, and deployable anywhere. We believed we would know when we got there.

That led us to some unconventional choices:

- Wasp has its own declarative language for describing a web app at a high level (the "Spec"), and its own compiler. [The Spec now lives in TypeScript](/blog/2026/06/15/wasp-typescript-spec), so you write it in the language you already use.
- Our definition of "full-stack" goes wider than usual for JS frameworks: frontend, backend, database, deployment, and everything that falls in between.
- Even though Wasp is built on React + Node + Prisma today, the machinery was designed from day one to support other stacks, thanks in large part to that same Spec and compiler.

Five years of that got us [over 100 releases](https://github.com/wasp-lang/wasp/releases), [users](/#showcases) ranging from startups to Fortune 500 companies, $5M raised, and a team of 8 plus everyone in the community ([waspeteers](/#waspeteers)).

It also meant a lot of people shipped production apps on a framework that technically told them not to. You know who you are, and we could not have stopped you.

## The picture is clear now

So what makes Wasp ready for 1.0? Four things changed.

- We found the right shape for [the "Spec"](/blog/2026/06/15/wasp-typescript-spec) without giving up on the vision. This was the big one.
- We developed an intuition for how to balance managed experience with flexibility.
- We collected a lot of feedback on every major feature (Auth, RPC, Jobs, Routing, API, Entities, Deployment) and we know what people need and how to get there.
- We are fully committed to [Full Stack Modules](/blog/2026/06/22/javascript-still-cant-ship-a-full-stack-module), the crown of the fullstackiest experience we are aiming for, and we are confident we can deliver them.

We can now see the exact road, paved with GitHub issues, to Wasp 1.0, and it feels right.

## What is shipping between now and 1.0

The full breakdown lives on [the roadmap](https://github.com/orgs/wasp-lang/projects/5). Here are the four you are most likely to care about.

### Auth, reworked

Auth goes through the biggest change. We are moving off Lucia and adding the features you have been asking for: account merging, 2FA, magic links, passkeys. We are also reshaping it into a modular implementation, so alongside Wasp Auth you will be able to plug in BetterAuth, Clerk, WorkOS, or anything else that implements our auth interface.

### One server, one command

We are simplifying `wasp build` to produce a single server, which unlocks a lot downstream. We are also picking the smoothest free hosting option out there and adding it to `wasp deploy <provider>`, while making Fly and Railway more robust.

### Native Windows

Wasp runs on Windows through WSL today. For 1.0 it runs on Windows directly, which is what you would expect from a 1.0 framework.

### Full Stack Modules

The one we are most excited about: the ability to define and distribute modular, vertical slices of your app. Stripe payments, file upload, AI chat. Imagine a library, but one that spans frontend, backend, database, Spec, and eventually deployment, all type-safe and configurable. We wrote about the idea in [JavaScript still can't ship a full-stack module](/blog/2026/06/22/javascript-still-cant-ship-a-full-stack-module).

[Full Stack Modules](https://github.com/wasp-lang/wasp/issues/2873) will reshape how Wasp apps get built, and we want that reshaping to land inside 1.0 rather than right after it, so the API settles around it.

### And the unglamorous half

A 1.0 is mostly made of work nobody writes blog posts about: a long tail of DX fixes (better support for agent orchestrators, cleaner terminal output, better docs, faster `npm install`), the release policy above, and proper documentation on performance and scaling so it is clear where Wasp stands and what you can rely on.

The roadmap breaks all of this down into [general polish](https://github.com/wasp-lang/wasp/issues/2884), [main features](https://github.com/orgs/wasp-lang/projects/5/views/1?filterQuery=label%3A%22major+feature%22), and [production-level work](https://github.com/wasp-lang/wasp/issues/2888), each with its own issues.

## What comes after

We are not done innovating just because we are closing in on 1.0. RBAC, a modular database layer that would open the door to Drizzle, and Wasp Studio (a visual view of your codebase plus dev tooling) are all things we want to build. They live in the [Ideas column](https://github.com/orgs/wasp-lang/projects/5/views/1?filterQuery=status%3AIdeas), and some will have early experiments by the time 1.0 lands.

## See you there

If there is something you want in Wasp 1.0, tell us on [Discord](https://discord.gg/rzdnErX) or [GitHub](https://github.com/wasp-lang/wasp/issues). The roadmap is public and we are still shaping it.

The destination is locked in. Next stop, Wasp 1.0.
